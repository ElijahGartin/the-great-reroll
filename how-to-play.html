The game in 60 seconds

Everyone gets three random character cards. Your goal is to finish with one character you want to play.

  1. Reveal three cards.Each card is a race/class combination. These are your starting options.
  2. Roll for turn order.Everyone rolls D100. Highest goes first.
  3. Make your choice.Claim one card, or use your one reroll before claiming.
  4. Give others a chance.Everyone eligible passes or submits a secret attack bonus and an offered card.
  5. Resolve the claim.No challengers? It's yours. Otherwise, choose defense and roll D100 + bonus.
  6. Lock it & keep moving.The winner keeps the character. Two unused cards go back into the pool.
One budget. Real choices. The default is 100 points per player, shared between attack and defense. Attack up to 50; defend up to 75. All committed points are spent, win or lose.

There is no single overall winner. The payoff is a full guild roster—and the claims, challenges, and ridiculous rolls along the way.

STAGE ONE · THE DEAL

1. Reveal your starting hand

The host sets up the players, faction, allowed combinations, copy limit, and starting points. Each player receives three cards from three different classes. The slot-machine reveal shows your assigned hand.

  1. When your name appears, the host presses Roll My Heroes.
  2. Read your three race/class combinations. You are not choosing your final character yet.
  3. Press Next Player until every hand is revealed.
How does a balanced deal work?

With “Balanced across the table” enabled, the deal spreads classes across the group within the enabled pool and copy limits. It also favors different race/class combinations before repeats. Every hand still contains three different classes. This does not guarantee a particular character for anyone, or a specific tank/healer/DPS composition.

The hand is a race/class choice—not a talent specialization. Decide your build separately.

Once everyone has spun

The full table is now visible. Press Enter Selection Round to roll for order. Finishing the deal does not mean your final characters are locked.

STAGE TWO · ROLL FOR ORDER

2. Let the dice set the order

The banner names the player who rolls next. Grab the die, drag it, and release to throw—or use Roll D100, tap the die, or focus it and press Enter. The host can roll on your behalf. D100 means a random whole number from 1 to 100.

  • The highest roll gets the first selection turn.
  • Tied players reroll only to settle their tied positions. Players above or below that tie keep their places.
  • No attack or defense points are used for turn order.
Going later does not stop you from challenging an earlier claim. Anyone who has not locked a character can challenge when eligible.
STAGE THREE · CLAIM YOUR CHARACTER

3. Keep your hand—or take one reroll

When the banner shows your name, inspect your three cards. You can claim a favorite immediately, or use your one reroll first.

Using your reroll

  1. Check Replace this card on one, two, or all three cards.
  2. Leave favorites unchecked. Those cards stay in your hand.
  3. Press Replace selected cards. Replacements are drawn from the remaining pool.
  4. Choose from your new hand. The reroll is final, and you cannot use it again.

You keep three different classes. Your discarded combinations cannot come straight back in that same reroll. The app draws replacements first, then adds your discarded cards to the pool.

Why the pool matters: whenever someone locks a character, two unused cards return to the pool. The next player can try their one reroll for those newly available options. A reroll is random—you cannot directly pick a card from the pool.

If the reroll button is disabled after you select cards, there may not be enough valid replacements. Try replacing fewer cards or keep your current hand.

Making a claim

Press Claim character under the hero you want. That card becomes the current claim. It is only yours permanently after the claim resolves.

A CLAIM IS ON THE TABLE

4. Challenge it—or let them have it

Each eligible player who has not locked a character submits one response. The app works through those players and counts how many are ready.

Pass

Let this claim go. You spend nothing, keep your cards, and can challenge a later claim if you are still unlocked.

Challenge

Enter a whole-number attack bid from 0 to 50, limited by your remaining points. Choose a card from the offered-card dropdown, then press Seal attack bid.

Your attack bid adds that many points to your D100 result. The offered card is what the owner receives only if you win. If you lose, your cards stay with you, but your attack points are spent.

The bid is a dice bonus. A larger bid improves your result; it does not automatically win the character.

Keep bids private until reveal

The host collects each response privately—for example, through Discord DMs—and enters it into the shared-screen game. Amounts are masked and submitted responses remain hidden until everyone has responded. Once submitted, that response is locked.

After all eligible players respond, press Reveal responses. Every submitted attack bid is deducted at this point, including bids that eventually lose. The defender then sees the challengers and their bonuses.

Why can’t everyone challenge every card?
  • Players who already locked a character are finished.
  • The owner cannot challenge their own claim.
  • A player already holding the same race/class combination cannot challenge for another copy.
  • The offered card must preserve three different classes in the owner’s hand if the challenger wins. The dropdown only includes eligible offers.

That is why the response count may be smaller than the total number of other players.

BIDS REVEALED

5. Choose defense, then roll

If there are challengers, the owner gets the last spending decision. Read the revealed attack bonuses, enter a defense bonus, and press Lock defense → Take turns rolling.

  • You may defend with 0 to 75 points, limited by what you have left.
  • You see the attack bids before choosing defense, but nobody sees the dice results before committing.
  • Defense points are spent whether you keep the character or lose it.
D100 roll + committed bonus = your total

The owner and each challenger take turns throwing the die. All bonuses are already locked, so they cannot change after seeing an earlier roll. Each result stays visible until you continue. The highest total wins. Sound is optional, and Skip animation skips the presentation without changing the result.

Read the roll colors

The raw D100 result uses Warcraft Logs–inspired colors: gray 1–24, green 25–49, blue 50–74, purple 75–94, orange 95–98, pink 99, and gold 100. Bonuses and totals are shown separately. A gold raw roll can still lose to a higher total.

Dragging is for the tabletop feel: throw strength and direction do not change the random odds.

How ties work

  • Owner tied for highest: the owner wins.
  • Only challengers tied for highest: those challengers reroll D100, with no extra points and no bonuses on the tiebreaker.
  • If the challengers tie again, they reroll again until there is a winner.
CHARACTER SECURED

6. Resolve the claim

If everyone passes

The owner locks their chosen character immediately. Nobody spends points or rolls a contest. The owner’s other two cards return to the pool, and the next unlocked player gets their turn.

If the owner wins the contest

The owner locks the claimed character and returns their other two cards to the pool. Challengers keep all their cards. Everyone who committed points has spent them. Continue to the next unlocked player.

The result stays on screen until the host presses Continue. Take a moment to read the exchange and the new pool before moving on.

THE GUILD IS READY

7. One final hero each

Repeat claims and challenges until every player has a locked character. Anyone who won a challenge earlier is skipped when their original turn comes around.

The final gallery shows each player and their character. Press Save Guild Roster · CSV to download the player, faction, race, and class list.

Your leftover points have no final score value. They were a resource to help you secure your character. Once you lock a hero, your game decisions are finished—but you can keep watching the rest of the guild fight it out.

KEEP THIS HANDY

Points & tricky rules

Default rules at a glance
RuleWhat it means
100 starting pointsEach player has their own budget, shared between their attack and defense spending. The host can change the starting amount in setup.
Attack cap: 50Maximum per challenge, or your remaining points if lower.
Defense cap: 75Maximum per claim, or your remaining points if lower. Defense has a higher cap, but not a separate reserve.
No refundsAll attack bids are spent on reveal. Defense is spent when committed. Losing does not refund either.
One reroll per playerReplace 1–3 cards once, on your own turn before a claim. It costs no attack/defense points.
One locked characterLocking ends your participation in claims and challenges.
Owner wins tiesOnly tied top challengers need a free tiebreaker when the owner is below them.
If I spend 50 attacking and lose, can I still defend with 75?

No. With the default 100 starting points, you now have 50 left. Your maximum defense is 50. The 75-point defense cap is a ceiling, not a guaranteed allowance.

Can I challenge with zero points?

Yes. Enter 0, offer an eligible card, and seal the challenge. You roll D100 with no bonus. Passing is different: it removes you from that contest.

Can several people challenge the same card?

Yes. All eligible challengers enter the same contest, all spend their revealed attack bids, and each rolls against the owner and the other challengers. Only the winner’s offered card transfers.

Can I take a returned card directly from the pool?

No. Returned cards become eligible for random rerolls. Gold highlights the most recently returned cards. Cards still in someone’s hand and locked characters stay out of the pool.

Can two players end up with the same race/class?

Yes, if the host allows two copies of that combination. Each copy is its own card. With one copy enabled, that combination can be locked by only one player.

What if I lose my claim after already using my reroll?

You keep your turn and receive the winner’s offered card, but your reroll stays used. Choose again from your current three cards.

Does “Either” mix Horde and Alliance cards?

No. It randomly chooses one faction for the whole deal. Both enabled pools must have enough cards. The group can choose Horde or Alliance explicitly instead.

Can I change my mind after I submit a response?

A sealed response is locked. Check the attack amount and offered card before submitting. Rerolls are also final, so only check cards you are prepared to replace.

YOUR DISCORD GAME NIGHT

Let one person host

This version runs on one shared screen. Your friends make decisions in voice chat; the host operates the app.

  1. Set up the group. Click The Great Reroll on the home page. Follow Players & faction → Rules & pool → Review & start. Enter your group, agree on the settings, then press Reveal starting hands.
  2. Explain the goal. “Three starting cards, one final character. You can claim, reroll once, or challenge someone else.”
  3. Share the game window. Reveal hands and roll for order together.
  4. Collect each claim’s responses privately. Ask eligible players to DM “pass” or their attack amount plus offered card. Keep those messages off the shared screen.
  5. Enter every response, then reveal. Let the owner choose defense only after all bids are revealed.
  6. Pause on results. Say who locked the hero, which card transferred, and who is choosing next.
  7. Save the roster at the end. Share the CSV and celebrate the lineup.
Keep the game tab open. Closing the in-app panel preserves the current game in that tab. Refreshing or closing the browser tab clears it. Open this guide in a separate tab during a live game.

Masked bid fields hide entered amounts on screen. This is a host-operated game, not secure multiplayer with separate player accounts.
